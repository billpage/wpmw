I have four questions for you. Each is meant to find where we actually differ, rather than to win a point.

First, the fork on slide 15. If you mean that N-particle quantum mechanics is one-particle quantum mechanics in a bigger configuration space, then we agree, and what's left is only vocabulary. If you mean that the interactions between particles inside one world are formally like the interactions between worlds, the reading where many particles stand for many worlds, then slides 11 to 13 are my evidence that it fails beyond one particle, and I'd like to hear which of them you find unconvincing.

Second, on the 20th you mentioned your own two-particle many-worlds calculation, in Maple, from six or seven years ago, and a reference from October 2024 on the time evolution of Wigner functions for open systems, with a two-particle example. I'd like to see both. My question about the calculation is diagnostic. Was the signed object a configuration of both particles, a world, a row; or each particle separately, a column? And when two of them were paired, were they two particles, or two configurations?

Third, the Volterra sea and the bound-pair chemistry were developed for one particle, where every pair is a pair of worlds. With two particles, a P–N pair with a Morse well has two possible readings: a bond, two particles in one world, which would be ordinary classical chemistry; or a chord, two worlds, which is what a positon and a negaton are. Our model needs the second. If it's a chord, its separation has to range up to about the reach to carry the kernel, rather than stay inside one cell. And your elementary up and down actions, which move a world's momentum by plus or minus half the photon momentum, would for two particles have to be exchanges along a bond, with equal and opposite changes in the two particles' momenta, to keep the centre of mass dark. Does your construction do that?

And fourth, a request for the year-end paper. Its scope, from the Wigner equation, through the linear four-action model and the linear up-and-down model, to the nonlinear Volterra sea, is all one-particle. A short paragraph saying what the objects are for N particles would protect it from the misreading. "Classical within a world, quantum between worlds", with "particle" kept for the identity that recurs in every world, is the shortest version I know. I'd also offer again two things: Takabayasi's 1954 paper, section 3 (a), as the origin of the odd-moment picture; and the table of moments of W and S as the argument for why there are two species. In a multi-particle reading, both species are kinds of world.
