This is the table from Definition WC7 in step 25, the addendum we called "worlds suffice", with one row added at the top for what happens inside a world, so that both levels appear in one place.

The column to read is the second one, the number of worlds each relation involves. Only the first row involves a single world, and it's completely classical. Under the sea postulate (S′), as we amended it in step 24, an unpaired world streams under the full force; a world that is half of an aligned pair is blind to the force and moves inertially; and every world's clock winds at its Lagrangian rate. Nothing quantum happens inside a world. All of the interaction between particles, the bonds, the potential and the force, is in that one orange row.

Every other row is a relation between worlds, and all of the quantum dynamics is in those rows. A chord, between two worlds, is a phase relation. A reading is the interference sum of the chords around a reader, which the reader's banks integrate. Emission and absorption, involving three worlds, a parent and two others, are the events, and they're realised with persistent identity. Pair collisions and dark catalysis, with four worlds in two pairs, are the sea's own dynamics. And admissibility is a constraint on the whole ensemble.

This is the same commitment that Hall, Deckert and Wiseman make: that the interaction between worlds is fundamental. Their interaction is a force between neighbouring worlds. Ours is a phase relation across chords that sets the rates of contact processes, while the classical force stays inside each world.

The slogan is in the title: classical within a world, quantum between worlds. It's the most compact answer I have to your analogy. In a Boltzmann gas, the interesting relations are between particles inside one world. In our model, the interesting relations are between worlds, and the inside of a world is a classical N-body system.

There are three places where particle language tends to creep back, and none of them is worked out yet. Identical particles: a permutation of the labels maps a world to another world, so statistics would be a relation between a world and its permutations. Spin: a spin label for each world-particle is just another coordinate, and the phase between spin components would be a chord between worlds whose labels differ. And the density of worlds: a reading needs chords, so how many worlds there are is part of the ontology, not just a numerical parameter.
