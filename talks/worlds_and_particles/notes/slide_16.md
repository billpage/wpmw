This is a practical summary for planning: which parts of steps 1 to 24 we can take over as they stand, and which questions the one-particle ladder could not have asked.

The left column carries over. That's everything about the generator, taken as a statement about one point in a phase space of 2 d N dimensions. The compensated split, and the need for a soft horizon. The reach, as a regulator that sets the momentum resolution and costs event budget. The ledger's sum rule from Theorem N3, with the absorptive fraction at one half when there's no sink. The sea postulate (S′) with the step 24 amendment, where aligned pairs are blind to the force and change momentum only in pair–pair focus and defocus collisions, with their H-theorem. And the integrate-and-fire banks of step 23, which become one bank per bond.

The middle column needs two or more particles. These are questions, not just harder calculations: who owns a clock, a sign and a bank; what bonds do, since they make the centre of mass dark for a pair potential, make every event conserve total momentum, and decide which directions of a chord are read; locality without particle phases; entanglement and the particle marginals, including the fact that classical streaming entangles in problem Q and over-entangles in problem G; and identical particles and spin, which in an ontology of worlds alone have to be relations between worlds.

The right column needs high dimension. These are costs that one particle in one dimension can't show, though one particle in three dimensions partly would: coincidence, and the resolution of the sink, with a cost that grows rapidly as the cell shrinks; chance crossings; the cost of K1 with six relative dimensions, for four particles on a line, or twelve, for three in space; and whether the live sea reading of step 23 works along a bond's relative direction.

A suggestion for both of us: when we first try a new idea about the sea on one particle, let's note which column it belongs to, so that we know in advance which multi-particle check it still owes.
