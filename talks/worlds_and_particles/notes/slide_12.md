The second test is about entanglement, and these results come from the second addendum to step 25. N particles of unit mass on a line, in a harmonic trap, with every pair coupled by the same potential, start in the product of the trap's ground states. We measure entanglement by the purity of one particle's reduced state, which is exactly 1 when that particle isn't entangled with the rest.

Problem Q, on the left, is the Moshinsky model: a harmonic pair interaction, which is exactly solvable. A quadratic interaction has a zero compensated residual, so no world ever fires. Yet the purity falls to 0.683 for three particles and 0.655 for four, and then revives periodically. Positive worlds, simply streamed classically, reproduce it to within 0.0015 and 0.0025. So entanglement is not, by itself, a sign of quantum events. The worlds carry it as a correlation between their own coordinates: in the table of slide 4, a correlation between the cells of each row, which no single column shows.

Problem G, on the right, is a Gaussian-smoothed contact interaction, the form used in studies of a few bosons in traps. It has no closed form, and the reference is a grid solution of the Schrödinger equation, converged to 10⁻⁹ in purity. Here classical streaming over-entangles. Its purity falls to 0.606, while the exact purity dips only to 0.800 at time 2, and recovers to 0.879 by time 3. The events have to undo that excess. The bond-sum generator on a mesh does so, converging as one over the reach squared: at time 3 its purity error is −0.117, −0.039 and −0.009 at reach 2π, 4π and 8π.

That's the cleanest statement I know of the two levels. The classical dynamics inside each world creates correlations between particles, here too much of them, and the quantum relations between worlds correct them.

With one particle there's nothing to entangle, so this division of labour can't even be seen. A one-particle formal analogy can't tell us whether a model of the sea entangles correctly; only a two- or three-particle run can. This connects to the interest I raised in step 24, in entanglement from two-body interactions as a test of the dynamics. Problem G is that test, and the generator passes it on the mesh. What doesn't yet pass is the realisation with finitely many worlds, which is the subject of the next two slides.
