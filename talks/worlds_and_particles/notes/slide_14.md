So what can replace the contact sink? That's what step 26 and the world-crossings supplement asked. All the numbers here are for problem G, compared with the mesh, at time 3.

Proposition PC2 explains the sink's error. A sink that keeps a cell's signed moments up to some order changes a smooth observable by the cell size to the next power. The contact sink keeps only the net count, the zeroth moment, so its error is first order in the cell, just as we measured.

K1 resampling, in the middle, keeps the first moment as well. In each cell it keeps the majority sign's net worlds, and adds pairs of opposite sign that carry the first moment of the worlds it removed. Each pair sits at the dipole centre: the one point where it also matches the removed worlds' second moment along the dipole. So a lone pair comes back unchanged, and a dipole moves with the flow instead of being pinned to its cell. With the grid shifted every ten steps, K1 with cells of 0.7 leaves a purity deficit of 0.020, against 0.143 for the contact sink with cells of 0.35, at about the same population. That deficit is the same with ten times fewer worlds, so it's the rule's limit at that cell size, and it shrinks to 0.014 with cells of 0.5.

On the right, absorption. Realising an event by removing a nearby world of the other sign keeps the absorbed share between 0.31 and 0.44, below the one half that the ledger needs to hold the population on its own, and its bias is at least that of a contact sink twice the tolerance in size. Leaving part of each cell's population to absorption, with K1 correcting the first moment, doesn't help either.

And crossings. I also asked whether crossings of worlds in configuration space-time, rather than nearness in phase space, could decide when an event is realised as an absorption. For N particles, an absorption vertex is where three world-lines meet: the parent's, and two partners', coming in along the bond from opposite sides. But chance crossings are far too rare once there are two or more relative dimensions. Worlds that share a vertex in their past, kin by their record, supply a partner for one daughter in 18 per cent of events by time one half, but the theory forbids that mixed realisation. The case it allows, with partners for both daughters, turns out to be exactly an undo of an earlier event of the same parent, bond and channel, and that's under one per cent of events. And the flow brings kin back to an exact vertex only when it's harmonic.

So my position is this. Absorption at a vertex is part of the ontology's picture of what happens between worlds. Moment-preserving resampling is a numerical technique for correcting the first moment, not ontology. And K1 should leave a tunable signed population for absorption to use. On the 20th you said that your bound-pair recombination only worked after shrinking the arena inside each cell. That's the same problem met from the other side, and K1 is the numerical equivalent.
