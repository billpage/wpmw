This slide summarises the supplement on the chord action, which we added to the repository this week. It restates Definition WC7 with a single generating relation, and it turns bonds and banks from postulates into consequences.

Here's the relation. Take two worlds at configurations X plus Y and X minus Y, so that X is the chord's midpoint and Y is its half-length. Each world's clock winds at its Lagrangian, so the difference between their clocks is the difference between their actions. And the difference between their Lagrangians splits exactly into the two coloured parts of the equation.

The orange part, 2 m X-dot dot Y-dot minus 2 Y dot the gradient of V at X, is linear in Y. Its Euler-Lagrange equation in Y is m X double-dot equals minus the gradient of V: Newton's law for the world at the midpoint. That's classical within a world.

The blue part is the compensated residual, R of X and Y: the potential difference across the chord, minus its linear part. It's odd in Y, starts at the third power, and vanishes identically only for a quadratic potential. That's quantum between worlds. We checked the split symbolically for three particles with a general potential.

Taking the Wigner transform over Y turns the linear part into the Liouville force term exactly, and the residual into a real, odd kernel in the momentum jump. That's the same kernel as in the chord-reading tutorial, sampled on the channels of the algorithm specification. We checked that too. For x to the fourth, the kernel agrees with the one-term Moyal residual to 7.9×10⁻¹¹. For minus 6 times the hyperbolic secant of x, squared, the Moyal series converges when the chords are short, with relative errors of about 7 per cent, half a per cent and 0.03 per cent at the first three orders, but it fails when they're long, with errors above 100 per cent. So the kernel is the generator, and the Moyal series is only its expansion for short chords.

The bottom row says what is now derived. Bonds: for pair potentials the residual is a sum over bonds of a single function, so events move momentum along bond directions, the centre of mass is dark, and total momentum is conserved. A bond is a term of the potential, not an object. Banks: a bank integrates the kernel along its world and fires at each integer. A Poisson process at the same rate gives the same mean count, 6.956 for both in the demo, but a much larger variance, 6.96 against 0.042. So a bank is a choice of timing, not a relation between worlds. And clocks: for two worlds on one momentum row the kinetic parts cancel, so the misalignment of their clocks changes at a rate set by the potential difference across the chord. Under (S′) the dark pairs are blind to the force, so this holds exactly. A reader whose own momentum follows the force sees only the residual: the compensated split is the reader's view of the chord action.

So the ontology is now: worlds, each with a point, a sign and a clock; one generating relation, the chord action; its realisation as emission and absorption with persistent identity; the dark-sector rules, which move nothing in W; and admissibility. The chord action is the exponent of Feynman and Vernon's double path integral, in Keldysh coordinates, and Polkovnikov's truncated Wigner approximation is what you get by keeping only the orange part.
