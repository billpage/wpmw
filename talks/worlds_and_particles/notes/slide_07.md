This slide is Proposition WC6 in step 25: what a single world experiences, under (S′) with persistent identity. No world is ever created or destroyed. An emission ionises an aligned pair, and an absorption joins two unpaired worlds into an aligned pair. Each is a kink in worlds that already exist.

The picture is configuration space-time. Time runs up, and the horizontal axis stands for the whole configuration space, so each line is the history of a whole world, not of a particle.

On the left, a parent world, the heavy blue line, streams along a Newtonian arc. At the emission vertex, an aligned pair, the grey dashed line, dark, blind to the force and moving inertially, sits at the parent's point and momentum. The pair splits. Its positon kinks to the parent's momentum plus ξ, its negaton to the momentum minus ξ, and they leave the vertex along the bond direction, from then on as unpaired worlds that feel the full force. The parent doesn't kink. It's the reader, and the events it parents never move its own momentum. A world that only ever parents keeps one unbroken Newtonian trajectory.

On the right is the time reverse, an absorption. Two unpaired worlds of opposite sign, at the parent's momentum plus and minus ξ, converge along the bond and meet the parent at the absorption vertex. They kink back to the parent's momentum and carry on together as a dark aligned pair. The world-crossings supplement defines this vertex for N particles: three world-lines meet, the parent's and two partners', coming in along the bond from opposite sides.

Three items in the list on the right are worth dwelling on. Item (c) is where the two levels meet. An event is a relation between worlds, but the jump it makes lies along a bond, which is a direction inside a world: one particle's momentum goes up by ξ, the other's goes down by ξ, and the total momentum doesn't change. With one particle in an external potential there's no bond and no conserved total momentum, so this structure is invisible.

Item (e) matters for how we talk about clocks. All worlds share one Newtonian time. The phase clock accumulates the action along the world's own history, divided by ħ. Its absolute value is a gauge, and only misalignments across chords are physical.

And item (a), that the change in unpaired worlds plus twice the change in aligned pairs is zero, is the ledger's conservation law, consistent with Theorem S7.

In the one-particle ladder all of this was true, but it looked like particles being emitted from a sea. With N particles it's clear that what kinks is a whole world, and that a world-particle kinks only because its world does.
