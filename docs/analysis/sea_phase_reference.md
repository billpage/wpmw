# The sea as a phase reference

> Whether the residual-kernel weights, which the compensated algorithm reads from the potential as a precomputed field, could instead emerge from the world-particles themselves, and what the sea would have to be for that to work. Theorem L1 writes each weight as a sum over contacts, `K_q(x) = -(B dp/hbar) int dy U_res(x,y) w(y) sin(2 xi_q y/hbar)`, in which the prefactor `B = 1/(pi hbar)` is the sea's own density and the strip the kernel sweeps holds exactly one aligned pair, `B dp 2 y_max = 1`; but by Proposition L2 sampling those contacts impulsively costs about `10^5` contacts per unit time to match the exact kernel, because each weight is a signed interference sum whose cancellations are the point, while replacing the pinned density `B` by the actual ledger sea costs almost nothing. Theorem L3 shows why per-world phases are the natural carrier: at their midpoint two bodies' extended phases wind apart at their energy difference, which for partners in one momentum row is the kernel's own `U(x,y)`. Theorem L4 is the obstacle it meets: with species signs, every aligned pair cancels exactly in any pair sum, so the sea can serve only as a set of unsigned clocks — a local oscillator, invisible as amplitude. Definition L0 makes the required relation precise: the **sea lock**, each aligned pair's clock sampling its row's plane wave, which classical flow carries as a smooth phase field `theta = S/hbar` over phase space that folds only when projected onto position. A gas of random phases never locks under the event rules; a locked sea largely persists. Theorem L5 introduces **dark catalysis**, an interaction between aligned pairs at the kernel's own rate in which both members of a pair fire together and their deposits cancel exactly, so it moves phase and nothing else; by Proposition L6 it couples nearly the whole sea near the barrier, makes created pairs dark, and at reach scale maintains the lock against the damage events do — 0.470 against 0.461 with no events at all and 0.395 with co-located coupling. **Theorem L7 is the negative result.** A freshly locked sea dephases at a rate that is a force-free pair sum linear in `V`, reproduced to correlation 0.999, but the force it subtracts is the partners' rather than the parent's, so its third moment — the leading quantum correction — is about `-2` times the compensated kernel's: a sea streaming under its own forces is the wrong reference for the compensated vertex unless a known leg-force term is added. Sections 5 and 6 carry the figures and an interactive page of the phase field.

## 0. What this note asks, settles and leaves open

The compensated algorithm is exact for $`E`$ but its vertex consults a
precomputed field: the parent at $`x`$ reads $`K_q(x)`$, a transform of
$`V(x+y) - V(x-y)`$ over the whole reach. Step 20 showed that the phase the
phase-blind no-go demands is not absent from this, but carried by the kernel
and by the sign structure of $`W`$ (Theorem Y1, as corrected there). This note
asks the constructive question: could the weights be generated by
world-particles meeting one another, with phase as the carrier?

**Settles.**

- The weights are literally a sum over contacts with the sea (§1), and the
  sea's density is the integration measure.
- Sampling contacts impulsively is unbiased but prohibitively noisy (§2); the
  measure dependence is mild.
- Pairwise clock differences carry the kernel's $`U`$ with the kernel's own
  geometry (§3), but only as unsigned clocks, because darkness annihilates any
  signed sum over the sea (§4).
- What the sea would need is a phase lock (§5), which events can maintain
  once a reach-scale E-neutral interaction is added (§6).
- A sea that streams under its own forces dephases at the wrong kernel (§7).

**Leaves open** the eight items of §8, chief among them whether partner
clocks can be referred to the parent's frame (L-SP1).

**Inherits.** Theorem N2 (kernel of the observable map) from step 18;
Theorems Y1, Y3–Y5 from step 20; Theorem I5 (the winding of the misalignment,
$`\dot\mu = -U/\hbar`$) from step 12; the reach from step 11; postulates P1
and P2 from step 4.

## 1. Theorem L1: the kernel as a sum over contacts

Converting the kernel's discrete Fourier construction (compensated algorithm
specification §§3–4) to an integral over the half-separation $`y`$:

**Theorem L1.** *The weight of channel $`q`$ at $`x`$ is*

```math
K_q(x) = -\frac{B\,dp}{\hbar}\int_{-y_{\max}}^{y_{\max}} dy\;
U_{\mathrm{res}}(x,y)\,w(y)\,\sin\!\left(\frac{2\xi_q y}{\hbar}\right),
\qquad B = \frac{1}{\pi\hbar},
```

*with $`U_{\mathrm{res}} = V(x+y) - V(x-y) - 2yV'_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)`$ and
$`w`$ the reach horizon. The strip it sweeps holds exactly one aligned pair,
$`B\,dp\,2y_{\max} = 1`$, and each separation $`y`$ contributes to every
channel at once.*

*Verification.* Part A of `demo_sea_lock.py`, at $`x = -0.625`$: the
integral reproduces the code's $`K_1`$ to $`5.5\times10^{-7}`$ and $`K_8`$ to
$`2\times10^{-3}`$ (discretisation of the $`y`$ sum), and the strip identity
is exact.

Read as contacts, the four factors each have a world-particle meaning:
$`B\,dp`$ is the sea density in one momentum row; $`U_{\mathrm{res}}/\hbar`$
is the rate at which the potential winds a two-leg phase across separation
$`y`$, minus its uniform part; $`\sin(2\xi_q y/\hbar)`$ is the misalignment
at distance $`y`$ of a pair split by $`2\xi_q`$ and aligned at $`x`$ — the
daughter pair of Theorem Y3 — and $`w`$ is the reach. The prefactor of the
Wigner transform is the sea's density.

A single contact drawn uniformly over the reach is an unbiased estimator of
$`K_1`$ with $`\mathrm{std}\sqrt N \approx 0.90`$ against a mean of 1.076,
constant from $`N = 1`$ to $`10^4`$: 84 per cent relative noise for one
contact, not a sign catastrophe.

## 2. Proposition L2: contact noise and the measure

`demo_contact_kernel.py` replaces $`K_q(x)`$ in the ledger (Eckart barrier,
$`p_0 = 1.2`$, $`dp = 0.25`$, no clamp) against the exact mesh on the same
grid, whose transmission is 0.4237.

**Proposition L2 (measured).** *(a) Weighting each contact by the actual
ledger sea $`S(x+y, p)/B`$ instead of the pinned $`B`$ moves $`E`$ from 2.9
to 3.8 per cent error while the sea departs from $`B`$ by up to 42 per cent.
(b) Generating the weights from Poisson contacts at rate $`R_c`$ per cell is
unbiased but costs about $`1.7\times10^5`$ contacts per unit time to match
the exact kernel, and is unstable below $`R_c \approx 50`$. (c) A single
contact carries a force; only the sum over the reach is force-free.*

| $`R_c`$ | 30 | 300 | 3 000 | 30 000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $`\|E - \mathrm{mesh}\|/\|\mathrm{mesh}\|`$ | 3.65 | 0.585 | 0.207 | 0.070 |

The error falls as $`R_c^{-1/2}`$ beyond 300. The measure dependence is mild
because every weight averages the sea over the whole reach, $`\pm 25`$ cells.
It does leave a spurious force, a first moment up to 0.135 against a peak
event rate of 3.04, and $`\sum E`$ drifted to 1.0010, unexplained (L-SP8).

On (c): each contact's first moment reaches 0.25. Projecting each contact
along $`\xi_q`$ removes it exactly, leaves the mean kernel unchanged (to
$`3\times10^{-15}`$) and costs nothing in noise (0.201 and 0.069 at
$`R_c = 3\,000`$ and $`30\,000`$). Projecting along the pure-force kernel
instead is far worse (0.283 at $`30\,000`$, unstable at 300), because that
kernel's weights are large in the high channels.

The noise is not a technicality. The weight is a signed interference sum over
the reach, and the cancellations between separations are what make it; one
contact is one term of a Fourier series.

## 3. Theorem L3: what two clocks carry

With P1, each body's clock winds at the Lagrangian rate, and by P2 its
extended phase is $`\Phi_j(x) = \theta_j + p_j(x - x_j)/\hbar`$.

**Theorem L3.** *(a) For bodies $`i, j`$ at $`x \mp y`$,*

```math
\frac{d}{dt}(\theta_i - \theta_j) = \frac{1}{\hbar}\left[\frac{p_i^2 - p_j^2}{2m} + U(x,y)\right],
\qquad U(x,y) = V(x+y) - V(x-y).
```

*(b) Their misalignment at the midpoint, $`\mu_{ij} = \Phi_i(x) - \Phi_j(x)`$,
winds at the energy difference, $`\dot\mu_{ij} = -(E_i - E_j)/\hbar`$, while
both move freely.*

The potential enters the clock difference as exactly the kernel's $`U`$, with
the kernel's geometry — partners at $`x \pm y`$, vertex at the midpoint — which
is Theorem I5 appearing without being put in. Part (b) says it is visible only
when the kinetic difference does not swamp it, that is, for partners in one
momentum row: the kernel's own strip, $`B\,dp`$.

**Cost.** Accumulating a clock is one multiply–add per body per step
(0.13 ms at $`10^4`$ bodies, 17 ms at $`10^6`$). Using it needs pairs within
the reach: $`2n_p\nu`$ partners per body, so the cost grows as $`\nu^2`$.
Binning pairs by vertex and separation and transforming over separation gives
all channels at once: 0.11 s per step at $`\nu = 8`$ and 0.46 s at
$`\nu = 16`$ on one core. The physical sea in the Eckart window is only 244
aligned pairs; $`\nu`$ is the ensemble multiplicity.

## 4. Theorem L4: darkness annihilates signed pair sums

A pair sum over partner bodies with weights
$`\sin(\xi_q d/\hbar + \mu_{ij})`$ reproduces $`K_q`$ when every
$`\mu_{ij} = 0`$ — the kernel is the $`\mu \equiv 0`$ limit of the sum over a
uniform spread of separations. Including the species signs:

**Theorem L4.** *Any pair sum weighted by $`\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j`$
vanishes identically on aligned pairs: the two members of a pair share
$`x`$, $`p`$ and $`\theta`$ and carry opposite $`\varepsilon`$, so they
cancel term by term against every third body.*

The sea can therefore contribute to the kernel only as **unsigned clocks**: a
phase reference that is invisible as amplitude. The analogy is a receiver's
local oscillator, absent from the output and supplying the timing every beat
is measured against. In the kernel the two legs are one body's ket and bra,
both entering with a plus sign; in the world picture the partner must play
the bra, a reference phase, not a contribution to $`W`$.

This is measured directly in §5: with signs included, the pair-sum kernel is
uncorrelated with $`K`$ even for a perfectly locked sea.

## 5. Definition L0: the sea lock

**Definition L0.** *The sea is **locked** when each aligned pair's clock
equals its row's plane-wave phase at the pair's position, $`\theta_j =
p_jx_j/\hbar`$ up to a constant per row. Within a pair the members are
aligned ($`\mu = 0`$, dark); the lock is the further relation between pairs
in one row.*

The lock is a relation between different pairs, not within one: a **gas** sea
has every pair dark but pairs mutually random. Under classical flow each
locked row becomes a Lagrangian manifold whose clocks carry the action,
$`d\theta = p\,dx/\hbar`$ along the curve, so the lock is carried as a smooth,
single-valued phase field $`\theta(x, p, t) = S/\hbar`$ over phase space. It
never folds in phase space. It folds only when a curve is seen along $`x`$,
where two momenta sit over one place on two differently coloured patches; that
is where same-row partners stop being aligned.

![Locked sea: phase field over phase space](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/billpage/wpmw/output/figures/sea_lock_phase_field.png)

*The locked phase field (hue) with six sample rows (white), for the Eckart
barrier and a soft-core Coulomb well ($`Z = \varepsilon = 1`$) at
$`t = 0, 4, 10`$. At $`t = 0`$ the bands are the hyperbolas of constant
$`px`$. Rows below the barrier-top momentum fold back; slow rows in the well
spiral.*

![Locked against gas](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/billpage/wpmw/output/figures/sea_lock_locked_vs_gas.png)

The [interactive page](https://billpage.github.io/wpmw/interactive/sea_lock.html) shows the same field
with time, potential and sea as controls.

**Does a lock emerge, and does it persist?** `demo_sea_lock_particles.py`
evolves bodies with $`(x, p, \theta, \varepsilon)`$ — classical streaming,
Lagrangian clocks, ionisation and recombination from the compensated kernel,
kinks keeping clocks continuous — and measures the correlation of the unsigned
same-row pair-sum kernel with $`K`$ near the barrier, against a
$`\mu \equiv 0`$ control:

| | $`\mu = 0`$ control | gas | locked, $`t \le 1`$ | locked, at barrier | locked, late |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $`\nu = 8`$ | 0.80–0.91 | −0.16 to +0.10 | 0.78–0.87 | 0.25 | 0.41–0.57 |
| $`\nu = 16`$ | 0.95–0.96 | −0.13 to +0.10 | 0.90–0.96 | 0.54 | 0.60–0.76 |

A gas never locks: sea-clock coherence stays at its noise floor, and
recombination under phase continuity makes gray pairs (mean $`|\mu|`$
1.40–1.48, against $`\pi/2`$ for random), as Theorem Y4's measure-zero node
condition predicts. A locked sea largely persists, dipping while the packet is
at the barrier.

## 6. Theorem L5: dark catalysis, and maintenance

**Re-locking on kinks.** When a body kinks into a new row, set its clock to
the circular mean of that row's sea clocks within a window $`W`$, transported
to its position; on recombination both members take the parent row's value.
This is injection locking of each body to the sea (Adler 1946), and it
makes dark creation and lock maintenance a single rule: created pairs go from
random (mean $`|\mu|`$ of 1.48) to aligned (0.19 at $`W = 3`$, and 0.02 at
$`\nu = 16`$), which answers Y-SP2 in part. But a sea body is re-locked only
when an event near a free body picks it, at roughly
$`\Gamma \times`$ (free density / sea density): at most 12 per cent of the sea
near the barrier is ever touched, and the kernel correlation stays at noise.

**Dark catalysis.** Sea pairs are excluded as parents because their members'
deposits cancel in $`E`$; cancelling in $`E`$ does not mean nothing happens.

**Theorem L5.** *Let an aligned pair A at $`(x, p)`$ fire channel $`q`$ at
rate $`|K_q(x)|`$ with both members together: the positon member, orientation
$`t = \mathrm{sign}(K_q)`$, realised as ionisation of a partner pair C in A's
row, $`C^+ \to p + t\xi_q`$, $`C^- \to p - t\xi_q`$; the negaton member,
orientation $`-t`$, realised as recombination of exactly those two bodies back
to $`p`$. Then $`\Delta E = \Delta N = \Delta S = 0`$ in every realisation,
body momentum is conserved event by event, the kinks are simultaneous
opposite-species pairs of $`\pm\xi_q`$, and C leaves aligned. The event lies
in the kernel of the observable map (Theorem N2).*

*Proof.* The two deposits are $`\pm t`$ at $`p \pm \xi_q`$ and cancel row by
row; each member of C kinks out and back by the same $`\xi_q`$. Under phase
continuity alone the round trip changes nothing (the ramps of Theorem Y4 pivot
at one point); with re-locking at the recombination, C re-forms on A's phase.
$`\square`$

The rate is the kernel's own, $`\Gamma(x)`$ per sea pair, so no constant is
introduced, and it acts only where $`V`$ is non-quadratic — where the kernel
needs a reference. Three versions were measured: C co-located with A and set
to A's transported phase (**imprint**) or to the mean of the two (**mean**),
and C anywhere in A's row within the reach, set to the mean of transported
phases (**reach**).

**Proposition L6 (measured).** *(a) Dark catalysis couples 87–95 per cent of
the sea near the barrier and keeps created pairs dark. (b) From a gas,
co-located coupling builds coherent patches that coarsen outward but do not
reach the reach scale by $`t = 25`$. (c) Against the damage events do,
reach-scale dark catalysis maintains a lock to within error of streaming with
no events at all, while co-located coupling does not. (d) What none of them
repairs is the erosion by streaming itself (§7).*

Coherence of sea clocks (C/floor), same row, $`\nu = 16`$, imprint, from a
gas, by separation bin of width 1.57:

| $`t`$ | $`d < 1.6`$ | 1.6–3.1 | 3.1–4.7 | 4.7–6.3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 21.8 | 1.9 | 1.6 | 1.4 |
| 12.5 | 45.9 | 7.4 | 4.7 | 3.2 |
| 20.8 | 56.1 | 22.7 | 10.0 | 2.8 |
| 25 | 55.5 | 26.6 | 11.8 | 5.4 |

Maintenance from a locked start, $`\nu = 8`$, three seeds, mean correlation
of the pair-sum kernel with $`K`$ over $`4 \le t \le 25`$:

| run | late mean | at the barrier |
|---|---|---|
| A: streaming only, no events | 0.461 ± 0.012 | 0.31 |
| B: events, co-located dark catalysis (mean) | 0.395 ± 0.010 | 0.27 |
| C: events, reach-scale dark catalysis | 0.470 ± 0.043 | 0.39 |
| $`\mu = 0`$ control | 0.88 | — |

B falls below A by about five standard errors: local consensus builds
patches that override the lock's long-range gradient (sea-clock coherence
across the reach 46 → 13 in B against 40 → 28 in A). C matches A. Commands:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -u src/demo_sea_lock_particles.py --nu 8 --mode locked --no-events --t-end 25 --seed 11
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -u src/demo_sea_lock_particles.py --nu 8 --mode locked --relock-w 3 --dark mean  --t-end 25 --seed 11
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -u src/demo_sea_lock_particles.py --nu 8 --mode locked --relock-w 3 --dark reach --t-end 25 --seed 11
```

Seeds 11 and 12 and the default seed give the three rows. The dark-catalysis
analogues outside the project are spin-exchange collisions, which relax spin
phases between atoms without ionising them (Happer 1972), and gossip
averaging (Boyd et al. 2006); the synchronised state compared through
transported phases is a travelling twisted state (Wiley, Strogatz and Girvan
2006) rather than Kuramoto's in-phase state (Kuramoto 1975).

## 7. Theorem L7: the dephasing of a locked sea

Theorem I5 suggested reading the kernel off the sea: the potential pulls a
locked reference off lock, and for a partner amplitude
$`z = e^{i(\xi_q d/\hbar + \mu)}`$ starting at $`\mu = 0`$,
$`\mathrm{Re}\,\dot z = -\dot\mu\,\sin(\xi_q d/\hbar + \mu)`$, so the pull is a pair sum of
the form of Theorem L1 with no precomputed field. The exact rate settles it.

**Theorem L7.** *For same-row partners at $`x \mp y`$ with equal momenta,
immediately after re-locking, the midpoint misalignment winds at*

```math
\hbar\,\dot\mu_{ij} = U(x,y) - y\left[V'(x-y) + V'(x+y)\right],
```

*because each partner's momentum responds to the force at its own position.
The dephasing kernel so obtained has zero first moment — streaming removes the
classical force — but it subtracts the partners' forces rather than the
parent's. At leading order its residual is $`-\tfrac{2}{3}y^3V'''`$ against
the compensated kernel's $`+\tfrac{1}{3}y^3V'''`$, so its third moment is
$`-2`$ times the compensated kernel's.*

*Verification.* Part B of `demo_sea_lock.py`, locked sea on the row lattice,
Eckart barrier, bookkeeping frozen per pair at re-locking (midpoints move by
up to $`v\,\delta t`$, and letting pairs change vertex cell swamps a change of
order $`\dot\mu\,\delta t`$):

| | $`\delta t = 0.02`$ | $`\delta t = 0.1`$ |
|---|---|---|
| corr(measured rate, Theorem L7 prediction), $`\nu = 8, 16`$, three seeds | 0.999–1.000 | 0.973–0.987 |
| corr(measured rate, $`K_{\mathrm{res}}`$) | 0.29–0.38 | 0.31–0.40 |

| $`x`$ | $`m_3`$ of $`K_{\mathrm{res}}`$ | $`m_3`$ of the dephasing kernel | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| −1.50 | 0.1396 | −0.2996 | −2.15 |
| −1.00 | −0.1863 | 0.3325 | −1.79 |
| −0.625 | −0.8514 | 1.6547 | −1.94 |
| −0.30 | −0.9473 | 1.8613 | −1.96 |

So a sea that streams under postulate (S) is the wrong reference for the
compensated vertex: it measures the kernel relative to its own members'
accelerations. The difference is the known term
$`y[V'(x-y) + V'(x+y) - 2V'(x)]`$, which the vertex could add, or which would
vanish if partner clocks were transported in the parent's frame (L-SP1). The
same erosion caps every maintained lock in §6: with no events at all the
correlation settles near 0.46.

## 8. Open items

- **L-SP1.** Can partner clocks be referred to the parent's frame, so that
  the sea's dephasing is the compensated kernel? Or is the leg-force term of
  §7 a vertex term to be counted as a postulate?
- **L-SP2.** Reach-scale dark catalysis: how is the partner chosen — uniformly
  within the reach, or weighted by $`w(y)`$ — and is it a postulate of the
  same standing as the kernel's own reach?
- **L-SP3.** The particle model's event dynamics is incomplete: 20–45 per cent
  of events find no partner in the co-location bin, and $`E`$ is not checked.
  Repeat §§5–6 on the exact integer ledger (cf. Y-SP5).
- **L-SP4.** Does co-located coupling coarsen to the reach scale at long times
  from a gas (the front advances about one bin every 6–8 time units)?
- **L-SP5.** Is a locked sea an admissible initial state — a boundary
  condition, the sea's "vacuum" — so that emergence need not be shown?
- **L-SP6.** The status of the sea at the vertex as unsigned clocks while it
  stays dark as amplitude (Theorem L4): a new postulate, or a reading of P2?
- **L-SP7.** Is any locked reference maintainable inside a potential under
  (S), or must the reference be blind to $`V`$? The ceiling near 0.46 in §6.
- **L-SP8.** The drift of $`\sum E`$ to 1.0010 in the sea-weighted kernel run
  of §2, where every event deposits $`\pm 1`$.
- **Y-SP2** of step 20 is answered in part: re-locking makes dark creation and
  lock maintenance one rule (§6). **Y-SP1** is sharpened by step 21's
  Proposition M9 and by the co-location bins used here.
