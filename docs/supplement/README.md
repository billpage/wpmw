algorithm specifications in `docs/algorithm/`.

- **[`phase_space_crystal_lattice_4d_supplement.md`](phase_space_crystal_lattice_4d_supplement.md)** — Companion document

  cases: two particles in 1 spatial dimension, and one particle in 2 spatial
  dimensions. Derives both jump rules from the multi-dimensional QLE,

- **[`phase_alignment_interaction_diagrams.md`](phase_alignment_interaction_diagrams.md)** — Every elementary process of
  the phase-alignment layer drawn twice, in the format of §4 of the
  crystal-lattice supplement: once as a lattice picture in the `(x, p)` plane
  and once as a set of trajectories in the `(x, t)` plane. Covers the free
  leg, the pump, the two exchange directions `s = ±1`, and the suppressed
  channels, and argues in §6 that the list is complete. §0.1 records why the
  phrase "wave–particle interaction" does not survive into this layer — no
  quantum is absorbed or emitted, and every momentum-changing event is a
  two-body exchange between world-particles. §7 records a sign discrepancy
  exposed by drawing the pump: the kick sign of §6 of the algorithm
  specification and the quadrature of Lemma 5 differ by one factor of −1, and
  the likeliest home for it is the unresolved direction label `s`. Figures
  generated by `src/gen_phase_alignment_interaction_diagrams.py` and published
  to the `output` branch.
- **[`four_action_foundations.md`](four_action_foundations.md)** — Reading of Cyganski's note *A journey from
  Bohm trajectory theory, through Nelson's SDEs and Wigner Particles to the
  Closed Four Action Model* (3 August 2026) against the derivation ladder in
  [`../analysis/`](../analysis/README.md), focused on the note's closing question: do momentum and
  energy balance determine the four-action rates? Page 4 of the note closes
  open item 1 of [`four_rule_microdynamics_equivalence.md`](../analysis/four_rule_microdynamics_equivalence.md) — the reconstructed
  hop rate matches exactly. The answer to the closing question is no:
  Proposition F1 shows conservation is *implied* by QLE-exactness for every
  member of the rate family, and Proposition F2 shows the focus gain is
  invisible to moments 0–2 and first appears at moment 3, because it multiplies
  the first quantum correction. Theorem F3 identifies the true selection
  principle as endpoint locality — Cyganski's own boxed remark that the
  intermediate-state density no longer weights the actions — which selects the
  model uniquely on its own. Proposition F4 isolates the residual quantum input
  to a single postulate: the momentum grid step is half the photon momentum of
  the mode; below it the model is classical Liouville, away from it Moyal at
  the wrong Planck constant. §2.1, added after review, separates the
  world-particle ensemble from the many-interacting-worlds models of
  Hall–Deckert–Wiseman and Poirier: granularity is not what removes density
  derivatives — carrying independent momenta is. Figures generated by
  `src/demo_four_action_foundations.py` and published to the `output` branch.
- **[`inverted_pair_barrier.md`](inverted_pair_barrier.md)** — Two particles with an *inverted* harmonic
  pair interaction, −(m ω²/2)(x₁−x₂)², specified as a test case.  Unlike the
  confining trap this is genuine scattering: the centre of mass is free, the
  relative coordinate is an inverted oscillator at reduced mass μ = m/2 and
  Lyapunov rate Ω = √2 ω, and although the Moyal bracket still truncates —
  so the QLE is *exactly* classical Liouville — the transmission probability
  is a quantum number with the closed form T = Φ(ū/σ_u).  The half-plane
  u = r + p_r/(μΩ) > 0 is exactly invariant, so T is a conserved functional
  of the Wigner function, readable at any time; that is what makes the test
  practical despite the escape problem.  Corrects §5.7 of the 4-D
  microdynamics note: the inverted barrier is a better probe but not a
  cheaper one, since reversing the potential's sign leaves every |V_q|
  unchanged and the per-mode noise constant identical.  Also gives the
  entanglement law S ≃ Ωt + ln t generated from a product state — the
  observable a mean-field solver returns as identically zero.

- **[`representation_cost_and_annihilation.md`](representation_cost_and_annihilation.md)** — Quantitative companion to
  §2.1 of [`four_action_foundations.md`](four_action_foundations.md), and the
  project's answer to A. Hackebill and B. Poirier, *On Hydrodynamic
  Formulations of Quantum Mechanics and the Problem of Sparse Ontology*
  (arXiv:2602.21106). Definition R1 reduces the cost of every representation in
  the ladder to one number, the L1 norm of the object sampled. Proposition R2:
  a cat state costs a bounded factor `1 + 2/pi = 1.637`, saturating — a bigger
  cat is not a more expensive cat — and Corollary R2.1 locates 38.9% of the
  ensemble in the interference lobe, where the probability density is
  numerically zero and where MIW has no worlds at all. Proposition R3:
  interference consumes momentum resolution, not position density, and
  Corollary R3.1 shows the specification's lattice `dp = pi hbar / L` is
  critically sampled for every state that fits the box — the Nyquist reading of
  Proposition T2 of [`takabayasi_1954_stochastic_picture.md`](takabayasi_1954_stochastic_picture.md). Corollary R4.1:
  entanglement is free (every Gaussian has L1 norm 1, Hudson) and only
  non-Gaussianity is charged for. Proposition R5 prices the pair `(x, mu)`
  ensemble as the Wigner ensemble with the Y-integral left to Monte Carlo, at a
  cost diverging like `1/dx`. Proposition R6 prices the positon/negaton sea at
  `1 + 2A/h`, which for the spec's grid is *exactly* the number of momentum
  cells, and Corollary R6.2 identifies the crystal condition with one
  world-particle per phase-space cell. §7 measures the annihilation burden for
  the first time — pathwise L1 growth `4 V_q / (pi hbar)` against a bounded
  `||W(t)||_1` — and finds two density criteria that disagree by two orders of
  magnitude. §8 lists five next steps; §9 argues the sparse-ontology mechanism
  does not transfer, because the kinetic dynamics divides by nothing, while a
  different threshold does. Figures generated by
  `src/demo_representation_cost.py` and published to the `output` branch.

- **[`emission_and_absorption.md`](emission_and_absorption.md)** — Tutorial
  introduction to the two realisations of a residual-channel event, written to
  stand alone for a reader who has not met the signed ensemble or the sea.
  Unpacks §5.3 of [`../algorithm/compensated_liouville_algorithm.md`](../algorithm/compensated_liouville_algorithm.md): because the
  observable `E = u+ - u-` is a *difference*, depositing `+1` at a daughter
  momentum row can mean adding a positon or removing a negaton, and the two
  readings are indistinguishable in `E` and opposite in every other book.
  Theorem J1: the pair count `P = S + N/2` is conserved *exactly* by the event
  channel at any absorptive fraction — verified to 3.5e-16 with transport and
  the bilinear sink both active — so `f` is not a rate constant in a
  two-population system but the partition parameter of a single one, which is
  why one value of it closes both ledgers. Theorem J2: that identity is
  **global only**; cell by cell it is violated 4e9 times more strongly,
  because an event debits the sea at the parent row and credits bodies at the
  daughters, so no degree-of-freedom argument from J1 constrains the local
  dynamics. §7 gives the thermostat reading of the `f = 1/2` attractor and
  tests it by re-clocking the relaxation against cumulative events, which
  collapses four rate regimes differing fifteenfold in `Γ` onto one curve. §9.1
  answers the ontological objection that ionising a sea pair teleports its
  constituents between momentum rows — a bound pair is one neutral object whose
  constituents have no worldline until birth. §9.2 records open item J-SP2:
  the `u± ≥ 0` clamp inside the allocation loop is repairing spectral-transport
  ringing in the wrong place, degrading J1 to 1e-7 and putting a 1e-2 floor
  under the ledger's independence from the observable, which converges cleanly
  as O(dt) with the clamp off. §8 withdraws support for the anti-correlated-leg
  explanation of the conjunction cost (J-SP1). Figures generated by
  `src/demo_emission_and_absorption.py` and published to the `output` branch.

- **[`takabayasi_1954_stochastic_picture.md`](takabayasi_1954_stochastic_picture.md)** — Close reading of T. Takabayasi,
  *The Formulation of Quantum Mechanics in terms of Ensemble in Phase Space*,
  Prog. Theor. Phys. **11** (1954) 341, §3, against the derivation ladder.
  Lemma T1: for a single Fourier mode Takabayasi's transition kernel `J` is a
  pair of opposite-signed deltas at `±ħk_q/2`, so his collision operator is
  *identically* the four-action stencil `Γ_q(x)(W_{n+q} − W_{n−q})` of
  [`../analysis/four_rule_microdynamics_equivalence.md`](../analysis/four_rule_microdynamics_equivalence.md). Proposition T2: for a
  potential of period `L` the support of `J` is exactly the momentum lattice
  `πħ/L`, which is also the exact Wigner support of any period-`L` state — the
  phase-space crystal lattice is the reciprocal lattice, not a discretisation,
  which removes the grid *step* from the residual quantum input isolated by
  Proposition F4 of [`four_action_foundations.md`](four_action_foundations.md). Proposition T3: the QLE
  stencil is not the generator of any one-body Markov jump process — negative
  off-diagonals, zero exit rate — and its elementary jump is half a photon,
  which is the defect behind Takabayasi's own footnote on `Ṽ(2(p−p₀))`.
  Theorem T5: a normalised, orthogonal, entrywise non-negative phase-space
  kernel must be a permutation, so the negativity he objects to is forced for
  every non-classical evolution and cannot be removed. §5.2 accordingly frames
  the project's answer as relocation rather than repair — sign becomes species
  and channel direction, zero net rate becomes a small bias on large sea
  traffic, and the half-photon becomes an interference midpoint of a
  whole-photon transfer. §6 recommends adopting his (3.17) as the compact form
  of the Kramers–Moyal hierarchy; §7 records two errata in the paper. Figure
  generated by `src/demo_takabayasi_stochastic_picture.py` and published to the
  `output` branch.
