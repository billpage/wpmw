# Weighted Wigner Paths and the Compensated Split

**A comparison note. Limkumnerd and Phanthaphanitkul have published a
carrier–residual splitting of the Wigner generator that is term-by-term the
same as this project's compensated split, reached independently and from a
different motive. This note establishes what corresponds to what, reproduces
their benchmark on our machinery, and identifies the one rung of the ladder
they do not climb — and what becomes visible only after climbing it.**

Reference: S. Limkumnerd and P. Phanthaphanitkul, *Weighted phase-space paths
for exact Wigner dynamics*, [arXiv:2605.05764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05764)
(2026).

---

## 0. What this note is

Expository and comparative, in the manner of
[`holland_two_fluid_correspondence.md`](holland_two_fluid_correspondence.md).
It asserts nothing normative about the algorithm. Its purpose is to place the
compensated split against an independent construction that reaches the same
algebra from a different direction, so that the parts of this project which
are genuinely new can be told apart from the parts which are rediscoveries.

Propositions here carry the prefix `W`. None is load-bearing for the ladder;
`W4` supplies an external check on Theorem E8 and `W2` sharpens a claim in
[`../../references/bibliography.md`](../../references/bibliography.md) that was
too generous as first written.

Companion demo: `src/demo_limkumnerd_weighted_paths.py`.

## 1. The split is the same split

Their construction takes the carrier process to be classical Hamiltonian flow
generated by the same $`H = p^2/2m + V(q)`$, so that the Wigner generator
splits as $`L_W^\dagger = L_{\rm cl}^\dagger + \mathcal{Q}_\hbar`$ with

```math
\mathcal{Q}_\hbar F \;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
  \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)!}\left(\frac{\hbar}{2}\right)^{2n}
  V^{(2n+1)}(q)\thinspace \partial_p^{2n+1} F .
```

The carrier carries the **full** $`V'`$ — no quadratic fitting, no preferred
piece of the potential — and the residual begins at $`n = 1`$. That is the
compensated split of
[`../analysis/compensated_liouville_splitting.md`](../analysis/compensated_liouville_splitting.md)
§2, and the stated motives are ours: a transparent classical limit, and the
quantum content confined to one channel.

**Proposition W1.** The two splits agree as formal series and differ as
operators only in what survives band-limiting.

*Proof.* Our symbol is
$`M = (i/\hbar)\bigl[V(x+y) - V(x-y)\bigr]`$ with $`y = \hbar s/2`$, and the
compensation subtracts the term linear in $`y`$:

```math
M_{\rm res} \;=\; \frac{i}{\hbar}\bigl[V(x+y) - V(x-y) - 2y\thinspace V'(x)\bigr] .
```

Taylor-expanding $`V(x\pm y)`$ about $`x`$ gives
$`V(x+y) - V(x-y) = \sum_{k\ \rm odd} 2 y^k V^{(k)}(x)/k!`$, and substituting
$`y = \hbar s/2`$ reproduces $`\mathcal{Q}_\hbar`$ term by term, the
subtracted ramp being exactly their $`n = 0`$ term. $`\square`$

The difference is one of form, not content, and it matters only later.
$`M_{\rm res}`$ is a closed finite difference: it is defined for any $`V`$ and
any $`y`$, whether or not the Moyal series converges, and in particular it
survives restriction to $`|y| \le y_{\max}`$ unchanged. A truncated series does
not. For the quartic benchmark below the distinction is inert, because the
series terminates; for a Gaussian barrier, where the series is asymptotic and
the differential form has no finite-order meaning, it is not.

## 2. Their benchmark, run here

Their configuration is

```math
H \;=\; \frac{p^2}{2} + \frac{q^2}{2} + \lambda q^4, \qquad
\psi_0 \;=\; \frac{|0\rangle + |2\rangle}{\sqrt2}, \qquad
t_f \;=\; \frac{\pi}{2},
```

with $`\lambda \in \{0.02,\, 0.05\}`$, $`dt = 0.005`$, $`dq = 0.0625`$ and
$`dp = \pi/48 \simeq 0.1309`$. This is, to the parameter, the configuration
of Part E of
[`../analysis/compensated_ontology.md`](../analysis/compensated_ontology.md).
The coincidence was not planned and is worth using: their published numbers
are an external check on ours, and ours on theirs.

Part B of the demo reproduces their runs. The classical carrier is
Strang-transported spectrally; the residual is applied as multiplication by
$`\exp(\tau M_{\rm res})`$ in $`(x,s)`$, which by Theorem C1 factorises
exactly against the classical kick.

| | classical carrier only | residual restored |
|---|---|---|
| $`\lambda = 0.02`$, published | $`5.7\times10^{-2}`$ | $`5.4\times10^{-5}`$ |
| $`\lambda = 0.02`$, here | $`5.673\times10^{-2}`$ | $`5.833\times10^{-6}`$ |
| $`\lambda = 0.05`$, published | $`1.1\times10^{-1}`$ | $`6.3\times10^{-5}`$ |
| $`\lambda = 0.05`$, here | $`1.081\times10^{-1}`$ | $`7.393\times10^{-6}`$ |
| harmonic null, published | $`4.8\times10^{-5}`$ | — |
| harmonic null, here | $`4.987\times10^{-6}`$ | — |

The norm is $`\|\Delta W\|_2 = (\int |\Delta W|^2\, dq\, dp)^{1/2}`$; their
convention is unstated but the agreement in the classical-only column fixes
it. The restored and null columns sit an order of magnitude below theirs
because our transport is spectral in both substeps and has no interpolation
floor.

![quartic benchmark](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/billpage/wpmw/output/figures/limkumnerd_benchmark_wigner.png)

One remark on what the restored column measures. For a polynomial $`V`$ the
Moyal series terminates (Theorem E7): here $`V^{(5)} \equiv 0`$, so
$`M_{\rm res} = i(\hbar^2/24)V'''(x)\thinspace s^3`$ exactly, and
"classical carrier plus leading residual" is not an approximation to the QLE —
it *is* the QLE. Their three-orders-of-magnitude improvement is therefore not
evidence that a weighted-path representation works; it is a check that the
arithmetic of an exact operator splitting is correct. The representational
question is untouched by it.

![error growth](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/billpage/wpmw/output/figures/limkumnerd_error_growth.png)

## 3. The residual is never taken to a measure

Their §VII writes the residual in kernel form once, schematically, as
$`\mathcal{Q}_\hbar F(q,p) = \int dp'\, K_\hbar(q,p,p')F(q,p')`$, and
immediately hedges: the precise kernel depends on the convention used to
separate the classical part. The kernel is never constructed. Every number in
the paper comes from applying $`-\lambda q\thinspace\partial_p^3 W`$ as a
finite-difference stencil.

This matters because of what their Fig. 3 then decomposes.

**Proposition W2.** The Hahn–Jordan decomposition exhibited in their Fig. 3 is
applied to the residual *field* on phase space, not to the momentum-transfer
*kernel*. The first always exists and is always finite; the second, for their
potential, does not exist at all.

*Proof.* Their Fig. 3 plots $`R_{\rm Moyal}(q,p) = -\lambda q\,\partial_p^3 W`$
and its parts $`\max(R,0)`$ and $`\max(-R,0)`$. This is a pointwise split of
an $`L^2`$ function on phase space; its total variation is
$`\int|R|\,dq\,dp < \infty`$ for any smooth $`W`$. The object a particle
representation needs is instead $`K_{\rm res}(x,\xi)`$, the density of rates
at which a world at $`x`$ receives momentum $`\xi`$. For
$`V = q^2/2 + \lambda q^4`$, Theorem E7 gives
$`V_W = V'\thinspace\delta'(\xi) + (\hbar^2/24)V'''\thinspace\delta'''(\xi)`$,
a finite-order differential operator, whose total variation is unbounded.
There is therefore no finite jump rate on the open line, and no positive
decomposition of $`K_{\rm res}`$ into event rates exists to be minimised.
$`\square`$

The bottom-left panel of the benchmark figure above is their Fig. 3(a) drawn
on our grid. It is a real object and the picture is correct: paired sources
and sinks concentrated on the interference lobes, visibly the negative of the
error the classical carrier accumulates. But it is the *effect* of events,
integrated against $`W`$ — not the event structure. Nothing in the paper
produces a rate at which a world jumps by an amount $`\xi`$, which is the
question the four-action microdynamics is about.

Their Appendix A4 has the same shape as this gap. They are careful to say the
minimality argument applies only after the residual kernel has been fixed, and
that different carriers give different kernels. What is not said is that for a
polynomial potential the kernel is not merely convention-dependent: until
something bounds it, it is not a measure.

## 4. Their grid instantiates a reach, and pays for it

**Proposition W3.** The momentum step of their benchmark fixes a reach
$`y_{\max} = 12`$.

*Proof.* The reach and the momentum resolution are one parameter,
$`\Delta p = \pi\hbar/(2 y_{\max})`$
([`../analysis/compensated_liouville_splitting.md`](../analysis/compensated_liouville_splitting.md)
§1.1). Their $`dp = \pi/48`$ gives $`y_{\max} = 12`$ directly. Equivalently:
their separation grid runs over $`|y| \le 24`$ in the full ket–bra
coordinate, and $`y_{\max}`$ is the half separation. $`\square`$

A third-derivative stencil on a grid of spacing $`dp`$ *is* a bounded jump
measure — it has finite total variation because it is a finite stencil. The
reach is present in their calculation. It is simply read as discretisation
error rather than as an ontological commitment, and so its price is never
quoted.

**Proposition W4.** On their benchmark, the accuracy of the restored
evolution saturates at a reach well below the one their grid instantiates,
while the residual event budget continues to grow cubically.

*Measured* (Part D, $`\lambda = 0.05`$, band-limiting $`M_{\rm res}`$ to
$`|s| \le 2y_{\max}/\hbar`$; budget
$`R_{\rm res} = \sum_q |K_q^{\rm res}|`$ at $`x = 1`$):

| $`y_{\max}`$ | 0.75 | 1.5 | 3 | 6 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $`\|\Delta W\|_2`$ | $`1.049\times10^{-1}`$ | $`6.886\times10^{-2}`$ | $`1.178\times10^{-3}`$ | $`7.393\times10^{-6}`$ | $`7.393\times10^{-6}`$ |
| $`R_{\rm res}`$ | $`0.431`$ | $`3.449`$ | $`27.59`$ | $`220.7`$ | $`1766`$ |
| growth | — | $`\times8.00`$ | $`\times8.00`$ | $`\times8.00`$ | $`\times8.00`$ |

![reach and budget](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/billpage/wpmw/output/figures/limkumnerd_reach_budget.png)

The error is converged to six figures by $`y_{\max} = 6`$ and does not move
thereafter. The budget holds $`\times 8`$ per doubling exactly — cubic, with
no saturation — which is Theorem E8's prediction for a quartic, here
confirmed on a potential and at parameters chosen by someone else. Their grid
sits at $`y_{\max} = 12`$ and therefore carries eight times the event budget
of a run that had already converged.

This is the sharpest way to put the difference between the two treatments.
On a deterministic grid the cost is invisible: a finite-difference stencil
costs the same whatever its total variation, so nothing in their numerics
could have told them which of these two columns they were paying. As soon as
the residual is asked to be a population of events, the second column is the
whole question — and by Theorem G5 the answer is not yet a fact of the
theory. Their construction inherits that defect without inheriting the means
to notice it.

## 5. Weights are not populations

Their representation is

```math
F_W(z,t) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\bigl[W_t\thinspace\delta(z - z_t)\bigr],
```

a positive carrier path carrying an evolving real-valued weight. This is
importance sampling in the Girsanov manner: the sign is an attribute of the
*estimator*, and it lives on a continuum. A path may carry weight $`-0.37`$.

The signed ensemble here is integer-valued — positons, negatons and sea
pairs, with births and deaths. The sign is a *species*, not a coefficient.
That is why this project has an absorptive fraction $`f`$, a conserved pair
count $`P = S + N/2`$ (Theorem J1), a sea population and an $`f = 1/2`$
attractor (Theorem S9). None of those quantities can be formulated in a
weighted-path scheme, because there is no population to be in equilibrium:
there is one carrier path with a number attached to it.

They do mention branching as an alternative — signed weights *or* branching
events — and cite the branching-random-walk literature. They do not construct
it. The branching reading is exactly where a demographic ontology would
begin, and it remains a parenthesis in their §VII.

## 6. Negativity versus admissibility

Their framing is anchored on $`W \ge 0`$ and Hudson's theorem: Wigner
negativity is taken as the signal that exact quantum phase space is not an
ordinary probability space. §0 of
[`../analysis/compensated_liouville_splitting.md`](../analysis/compensated_liouville_splitting.md)
corrects precisely this framing. A coherent or squeezed state has
$`W \ge 0`$ everywhere, evolves by exact classical Liouville flow in a
harmonic well, and is fully quantum. What is quantum about the initial datum
is the $`\hbar`$-sized floor on phase-space area — admissibility,
$`|W| \le 2/h`$ — not the sign.

They get halfway there independently. Their harmonic null case observes that a
state can be Wigner-negative while the residual generator vanishes, which
decouples negativity from *dynamical* nonclassicality. The converse — that
non-negativity does not buy classicality either — is not drawn, and so
admissibility never appears as the criterion.

**Proposition W5.** The Wigner $`L^2`$ error and the admissibility failure are
different diagnostics of the same omission, and the second is the stronger
statement.

*Measured* (Part C, $`\lambda = 0.05`$, least eigenvalue of the $`\rho`$
reconstructed from $`W`$ by the inverse Wigner transform):

| $`t`$ | exact | classical only | residual restored |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | $`-5.7\times10^{-16}`$ | $`-5.7\times10^{-16}`$ | $`-5.7\times10^{-16}`$ |
| $`\pi/8`$ | $`-2.3\times10^{-16}`$ | $`-3.48\times10^{-2}`$ | $`-7.1\times10^{-16}`$ |
| $`\pi/4`$ | $`-2.5\times10^{-16}`$ | $`-4.92\times10^{-2}`$ | $`-1.0\times10^{-15}`$ |
| $`\pi/2`$ | $`-3.3\times10^{-16}`$ | $`-4.96\times10^{-2}`$ | $`-1.8\times10^{-15}`$ |

![admissibility](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/billpage/wpmw/output/figures/limkumnerd_admissibility.png)

Their $`1.1\times10^{-1}`$ says the classical carrier gets the answer wrong.
The table says something stronger: the object it produces is not the Wigner
transform of any density matrix at all. That is Theorem G4, which reports
$`-4.97\times10^{-2}`$ at $`t = \pi/2`$ for this configuration on an
independent grid, with an independent implementation of both the transform and
the transport — reproduced here to three figures. The residual channel is
doing kinematic work as well as dynamical work, and no norm on $`W`$ can see
the difference.

## 7. What is theirs and not ours

The forward–reverse relation for signed Wigner path measures,

```math
\frac{d\mu_F}{d\mu_R^{\Theta}} \;=\; A_{\rm sign}\thinspace e^{A_{\rm mag}},
\qquad
A_{\rm sign} \;=\; \mathrm{sgn}(W_F)\thinspace\mathrm{sgn}(W_R^{\Theta}),
```

with the sign factor read as an interference parity rather than an entropy
production. There is no counterpart here, and it is orthogonal to everything
above: it is a statement about signed path measures under time reversal, not
about how the split is taken.

Whether it touches $`f`$ is worth asking and not worth asserting. Both are
exchange-symmetry statements about a signed channel, and $`f = 1/2`$ is the
fixed point of ours. But $`A_{\rm sign}`$ compares a path with its
time-reverse, while $`f`$ partitions events between the two realisations at a
single instant, and the formal resemblance may be no more than that. Recorded
as open item W-SP1; it needs a probe before it earns a proposition.

## 8. Summary

| | Limkumnerd–Phanthaphanitkul | this project |
|---|---|---|
| split | carrier + Moyal residual | identical (W1) |
| residual form | differential series | closed finite difference |
| reach | present in the grid, unrecognised (W3) | constitutive |
| $`\pm`$ split of | the residual field (W2) | the transfer kernel |
| signs carried by | real weights on one path | integer species populations |
| quantum criterion | Wigner negativity | admissibility (W5) |
| aim | representation theorem, path reversal | ontology and algorithm |
| forward–reverse | $`A_{\rm sign}`$ | absent (W-SP1) |

The honest summary is that they reached our §2 independently and stopped
there, because their question ended there. Their paper is about what kind of
stochastic object the Wigner function can be. Ours is about what happens to a
world. The compensated split answers the first question completely and the
second not at all, and everything on the ladder above it exists because of the
gap.

### Open items

- **W-SP1.** Whether $`A_{\rm sign}`$ and the absorptive fraction $`f`$ are
  related or merely both exchange-symmetric. Needs a probe, not an argument.
- **W-SP2.** Their $`\chi_{\rm Q}(t)`$ residual diagnostic is a norm ratio on
  the generator and is reach-independent. The event budget $`R_{\rm res}`$ is
  not. Whether some ratio of the two is the reach-free measure of residual
  activity that Theorem G5 asks for is untested.
