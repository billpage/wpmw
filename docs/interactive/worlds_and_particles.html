Is the one-particle case enough?

David

There is a formal analogy between many particles and many worlds, so working with one particle loses nothing.

Bill

With one particle a world and a particle are the same object, and that hides what the multi-particle model is.

Where the deck ends up

There are two analogies. N particles in d dimensions = one particle in dN dimensions is exact. Particles in a world ↔ worlds in an ensemble misleads.

A classical precedent: μ-space and Γ-space

Boltzmann: one world, N particles

N points in the 6-dimensional space of one particle. The lines are bonds: the particles interact through V. The whole picture is one world.

Gibbs: many worlds, one point each

Each point is a whole configuration in 6N dimensions. Gibbs’s copies never interact; WPMW’s worlds are signed and do.

For N = 1 the two spaces coincide, and a gas of worlds looks exactly like a gas of particles.

World = a row

A point of ℝ⁶ᴺ with one sign and one clock. Its banks are derived.

World-particle = a cell

Particle i in world w. No sign, clock or bank of its own.

Particle = a column

An identity in every world. Its state is the column’s signed marginal Wᵢ.

WPMW · worlds and particles · 4 / 20

Two kinds of pair, with the same formulas

Bond: two particles, one world

r = x₁ − x₂, p_r = ½(p₁ − p₂)
X = ½(x₁ + x₂), P = p₁ + p₂

The potential v(r) acts here. It gives the classical force inside the world, and it decides which directions a chord can read.

Chord: two worlds, every particle

worlds at X + Y and X − Y
μ = θ − θ′ + P·(x′ − x)/ħ

The misalignment of two clocks, read at the midpoint. This is where the potential acts beyond the classical force.

With one particle there are no bonds, so every “pair” we have drawn was a pair of worlds. The von Neumann equation for one particle is a Schrödinger equation for two configurations with H(x) − H(x′): the second “particle” has mass −m and potential −V. It is the bra world.

(a) Worlds and their signs are conserved: ΔN + 2ΔS = 0.

(b) Between events ẋᵢ = pᵢ/mᵢ; ṗᵢ = −∇ᵢV if unpaired, 0 if dark; θ̇ = L/ħ.

(c) An event on bond (i, j) moves pᵢ by ±ξ and pⱼ by ∓ξ. Positions stay continuous.

(d) The parent, the reader, never kinks at its own events.

(e) One Newtonian time for all worlds. A clock measures action, not time.

One relation between worlds: the chord action

ΔL = L(X + Y) − L(X − Y) = 2mẊ·Ẏ − 2Y·∇V(X) − R(X, Y)

two worlds at X ± Y; each clock winds at L/ħ, so ΔS = ħ(θ − θ′) (CA1, CA2)

Linear in Y: classical within a world

Euler–Lagrange in Y gives m Ẍ = −∇V(X): Newton’s law for the world at the midpoint, the Liouville term exactly.

Residual R: quantum between worlds

Odd in Y, O(Y³), zero for quadratic V. Its Fourier transform over Y is the whole event kernel Θ.

Labels or coordinates: where worlds are coupled

On the domain

Schiff–Poirier

A smooth label C, ρ = 1/x′. The coupling is (ħ²/8m) x″²/x′⁴, the Fisher information: four derivatives in C. Pure states only.

On label neighbours

Hall–Deckert–Wiseman

The same coupling as differences between neighbours: U_N/N → 1/8 for a Gaussian. Sorting finds the neighbours only in 1D.

On the target

WPMW

Chords X ± Y are differences of target points. R(X, Y) is linear in W and needs only midpoints and differences. Any state.

A label is constant along each world and never observed; a coordinate is what the action varies (CA0). Relabelling C is a gauge symmetry.

A pair potential reads only the chord’s extent along each bond:
R(x, y) = Σ_bonds ρ_v(r_b, η_b), η_b = yᵢ − yⱼ (WC9)

Bonds are terms of R, so one bank per bond, N(N − 1)/2 of them, sharing one kernel table, follows (WC4, CA5).

A chord that moves only particle 1 reads only the terms of V that involve particle 1 (WC8.1). Locality comes from chord geometry, not from particle phases.

Test 2 of the particles-as-worlds analogy

Entanglement lives inside worlds

Problem Q: Moshinsky, quadratic bonds

0 events

One-body purity falls to 0.683 (3 particles) and 0.655 (4). Positive worlds streamed classically reproduce it within 1.5×10⁻³ and 2.5×10⁻³.

Problem G: Gaussian bonds, 3 bosons

0.606 · 0.800

Classical streaming over-entangles (purity down to 0.606; exact dips to 0.800). The events between worlds remove the excess: within 0.01 at reach 8π on the mesh.

Entanglement is a correlation among the cells of a row; the quantum events correct it between rows. With one particle neither exists.

Population control is numerics, not ontology

Contact sink

−0.143

global purity deficit at t = 3, cell 0.35, ν = 10⁶. Keeps only each cell’s count M₀, so its error is first order in the cell (PC2).

K1 resampling

−0.020

cell 0.7, grid shifted every ten steps, 1.37 worlds per ν. Keeps M₀ and M₁, each ± pair at the dipole centre (PC3–PC6).

Absorption, crossings

< ½

absorbed share 0.31–0.44 with a tolerance (PC5). Chance crossings are too rare; kin re-absorption is an undo, under 1 % of events (CX3–CX6).

An absorption is a vertex where three world-lines meet: the ontology’s picture. K1 is what holds the population in practice.

The analogy that holds

N particles in d dimensions are one particle in dN dimensions. That one particle is a world.

The analogy that misleads

The particles inside a world, treated as if they were the worlds of an ensemble.

What the one-particle ladder gives, and what it can’t

Carries over, read as one world

  • the compensated split and the soft horizon
  • the reach as a regulator (G5)
  • the ledger: Γ(1 − 2f) = R_sink, f = ½
  • (S′) and pair collisions in the sea
  • integrate-and-fire banks, now per bond

Needs two or more particles

  • who owns clocks and banks (WC1, WC2, WC4)
  • bonds, a dark centre of mass, conserved P
  • locality from chords (WC8.1)
  • entanglement and particle marginals
  • identical particles, spin (WC-SP7, SP8)

Needs high dimension

  • coincidence and sink costs (WC10, M10)
  • chance crossings (CX3)
  • K1’s cost in six or twelve relative dimensions (PC-SP2)
  • live sea readings along a bond (WC-SP2)
