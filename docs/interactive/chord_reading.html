Events

    Postulate (S′)

    Free bodies obey the full force. An aligned pair, a positon and a negaton at one point with one clock, moves inertially along its row, but its clock still winds at the Lagrangian rate:

    ħ dθ/dt = p²/2m − V(x)

    So a sea pair keeps its row's plane wave except for the straight-line eikonal −∫V dt it picks up crossing the barrier. Watch the hue change as pairs cross. Switch to (S): pairs near the barrier bend off their rows and mix with their neighbours.

    The chord

    Two aligned pairs on one row, at x ∓ y. Under (S′) they move together, so 2y never changes, and their clocks drift apart at exactly

    ħ dμ/dt = V(x+y) − V(x−y) = U(x, y)

    That is the phase rate the potential applies to the density-matrix element ρ(x+y, x−y). The chord's two pairs are reference clocks at the two arguments of ρ, not the body's ket and bra.

    What the reading estimates

    The reader sums its row's chords near the vertex, each weighted by the daughter row's lever:

    K̂_q = −(B dp / ħ n² Δx) Σ w(y) · ħμ̇ · sin(ξ_q d/ħ + μ)

    Its mean is the Wigner–Moyal kernel Kq(x), the commutator with V sampled at the transfer ξq, with its classical part removed. It reads the operator and never the state. Press “Average” to watch the mean of fresh seas land on the blue bars; one reading at ν = 1 has a chord one time in twelve.

    From reading to event

    Each free body carries one integrator per channel, advanced by the rate and firing at every integer it crosses. A firing puts +τ at p + ξ and −τ at p − ξ. It is realised as an absorption if free bodies of the right species cross the vertex on those rows, otherwise as an emission that ionises a sea pair in the parent's cell. Feed the integrators from the live reading and they chatter: Q12's closed-loop failure.
