# AI use and authorship in this repository

This document expands on the "AI collaboration and authorship" section of
[`README.md`](README.md). It exists so that anyone relying on this
repository — as a reader, a collaborator, or someone deciding whether and
how to cite or build on it — knows concretely how AI was involved, what
that does and doesn't mean for the reliability of any given claim, and how
it bears on the licensing terms in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) and
[`LICENSE-DOCS`](LICENSE-DOCS).

Last reviewed: September 2026. Practice in this area — publisher policy,
copyright guidance, and community norms around AI-assisted research — is
still moving; this document describes current practice and current
guidance, not a permanent settlement, and may be revised as either
changes.

## Who does what

- **Bill Page** directs the research: he poses the physics questions,
  makes every ontological and design decision (what the model claims
  about the world, not just what the numbers say), decides what's worth
  pursuing or parking, and is the sole gatekeeper for every change —
  nothing lands in this repository without his own review and explicit
  action (`git am` against a patch he has read, not an automatic merge).
- **David Cyganski** is a collaborator who originated parts of the
  theoretical framework (the phase-space crystal lattice model, the
  four-action microdynamics framework) and contributes source memos and
  discussion.
- **Claude** (Anthropic) drafts analysis notes and derivations under
  Bill's direction, implements and executes the numerical code that
  verifies them, builds supporting artifacts (figures, notebooks,
  tooling), does comparative literature research, and proposes options at
  open decision points — but does not decide which of those options is
  adopted, does not set the research direction, and does not review or
  approve its own work as the final check. That role is Bill's.

This division — a human directing the work, making the judgment calls,
and reviewing everything before it's kept — is why Claude is credited at
the commit level (`Claude <claude@anthropic.invalid>` in the git history,
for provenance) but not listed as an author in `CITATION.cff` or in any
citation of this work. See "Why AI isn't listed as a research author"
below for the reasoning behind that line, not just the fact of it.

## What "verified" means here

A claim in this repository having been "verified" means it was executed
and checked against theory — run against the actual current code, not
just derived on paper, with the result checked for consistency with what
the derivation predicts. Where possible, claims are also cross-checked
against an independently-arrived-at formulation (for example, the Holland
bi-Hamiltonian correspondence connects two frameworks developed
separately, one of the actually more useful cross-checks available in
this kind of research). When a live-executed result disagrees with an
archived number quoted in a note — as happened when the
`emission_and_absorption` notebook exposed a small discrepancy tied to a
documented transport-code fix — the discrepancy is flagged explicitly
rather than silently reconciled, and the live result stands as current
ground truth until it's investigated further.

This standard is a real check against the specific failure mode the
research community is most worried about with AI-assisted work right
now — unverified, sometimes hallucinated, output making it into the
record unexamined (arXiv's 2026 policy tightening around AI-generated
citations is aimed squarely at that). It is not a substitute for external
peer review, which this repository has not had.

## Why AI isn't listed as a research author

Current guidance from the ACM's authorship policy, the Committee on
Publication Ethics, and most major publishers converges on the same
position: an AI system should not be listed as an author, because
authorship requires being able to understand what was written, consent to
its publication, and take responsibility for it if it's wrong — none of
which an AI system can do. What should happen instead, per that same
guidance, is disclosure: saying concretely what was AI-assisted and how it
was checked, rather than leaving it to be inferred or left unstated. This
document, together with the commit history, is that disclosure for this
repository.

## Copyright and the license terms

United States copyright law requires meaningful human authorship — work
generated by an AI system with no meaningful human creative control isn't
copyrightable at all, a position reaffirmed by the Supreme Court in a
March 2026 ruling declining to disturb it. Where a human directs the
work, exercises real editorial control, and makes the substantive
decisions — using AI more like a tool than a co-author, the way the
Copyright Office has analogized it to a camera or a word processor — the
human is the author and the resulting work is copyrightable normally.

The division of labor described above (Bill directing, deciding, and
reviewing every change; Claude drafting and implementing under that
direction) is the fact pattern that supports Bill's authorship of this
repository's content under that framework, and is why the existing
licenses — [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) (AGPL-3.0-or-later, for `src/`) and
[`LICENSE-DOCS`](LICENSE-DOCS) (CC-BY-SA-4.0, for `docs/` and
`references/`) — are unchanged and continue to name Bill Page as the
licensor. This is not legal advice, and it isn't a substitute for a
lawyer's opinion if that question ever matters for a specific portion of
this work; it's a factual account of the current legal landscape and how
this repository's actual working practice relates to it.

## For readers

Nothing here has been through external peer review. The verification
standard described above is real, and it is meaningfully more scrutiny
than most AI-assisted output receives before publication — but it is
internal to this project, done by the same people (and the same AI)
producing the claims. Apply the scrutiny you would apply to any
unreviewed research repository, AI-assisted or not, and see
[`docs/README.md`](docs/README.md) if you want to trace a specific claim
back to the note and the code that verify it.
